What Are Cognitive Functions?

Carl Jung originally described four psychological functions: Sensing, Intuition, Thinking, and Feeling. Each function can be directed either inward or outward, giving us eight different ways of approaching information and experience. These are commonly known as the eight cognitive functions.

Sensing and Intuition describe how we take in information, while Thinking and Feeling describe how we make judgments. Function

How Do They Work Together?

In MBTI type dynamics, each personality type is associated with a particular pattern of cognitive functions. For example, ISTP is commonly described through this function stack:

Ti → Se → Ni → Fe

This suggests that ISTPs tend to approach situations through their own logical understanding, while also paying close attention to what is happening around them.

But having a preference doesn't mean being limited to one way of thinking. A Thinking type isn't necessarily insensitive, just as a Feeling type isn't necessarily irrational.

Rather than defining what we can or cannot do, cognitive functions offer a way to reflect on our natural tendencies and how we approach the world.
